# Sustainability & Ethics

As digital humanists engaging with artificial intelligence, we cannot view computation as an abstract, ethereal cloud. Every generative query, neural model training cycle, and algorithmic prediction relies on physical infrastructure: massive data centers, cooling systems, mineral extraction, and thousands of gallons of freshwater.

## The Environmental Cost of Generative AI

- **Energy & Carbon Intensity**: Large language models and diffusion systems consume gigawatt-hours of electrical power during training and inference. Even simple queries carry a measurable energy footprint compared to standard algorithmic search.
- **Water Consumption**: Hyperscale server farms require significant evaporative water cooling, often drawing from drought-stressed municipal watersheds.
- **Hardware Lifecycle & E-Waste**: The rapid obsolescence cycles of specialized AI accelerators (GPUs and TPUs) fuel mineral extraction (lithium, cobalt, rare earths) and create persistent electronic waste.

## Human Labor in the AI Supply Chain

The illusion of automated machine intelligence is built on an invisible foundation of global human labor:
- **Data Enrichment & Content Moderation**: Low-wage workers across the Global South manually label training images, verify synthetic outputs, and filter toxic material, often enduring severe psychological tolls with inadequate support.
- **Cultural Extraction & Consent**: Mass training datasets scrape creative works, literary corpora, and personal expression from writers and artists without explicit informed consent, fair compensation, or credit.

## My Commitments in This Course

1. **Mindful Computational Footprint**: Using AI intentionally and sparingly—avoiding redundant batch queries or frivolous generative calls where simpler tools suffice.
2. **Promoting Local & Efficient Models**: Prioritizing smaller, open-weights, and quantized models where possible rather than relying solely on cloud-based mega-clusters.
3. **Rigorous Ethical Accounting**: Maintaining a public AI use log and acknowledging the human and ecological resources behind any AI-assisted work.
4. **Advocating for Algorithmic Justice**: Centering questions of power, environmental justice, and labor rights in both critical essays and practical making projects.
